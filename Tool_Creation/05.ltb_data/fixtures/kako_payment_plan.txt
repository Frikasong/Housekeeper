CAPREIT LIMITED PARTNERSHIP (the 'Landlord') applied for an order to terminate the tenancy and evict [Tenant 1] and [Tenant 2] (the 'Tenant') because the Tenant did not pay the rent that the Tenant owes.
This application was heard by videoconference on May 20, 2026.
The Landlord’s legal representative Rishma Nair and the Tenant [Tenant 1] attended the hearing.
Determinations:
1. The Landlord served the Tenant with a valid Notice to End Tenancy Early for Non-payment of Rent (N4). The Tenant did not void the notice by paying the amount of rent arrears owing by the termination date in the notice or before the day the application was filed.
2. As of the hearing date, the Tenant was still in possession of the rental unit.
3. The lawful rent is $2,357.14. It is due on the 1st day of each month.
4. The rent arrears owing to May 31, 2026, are $23,329.05.
7. I have considered all of the disclosed circumstances in accordance with subsection 83(2) of the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (the 'Act'), and find that it would not be unfair to grant relief from eviction subject to the conditions set out in this order pursuant to subsection 83(1)(a) and 204(1) of the Act.
8. The parties advised the Board that they had discussed a payment arrangement to resolve the rent arrears and had reached an agreement regarding the repayment schedule. Having considered the parties' submissions, I am satisfied that the agreed payment plan is reasonable and provides the Tenant with a meaningful opportunity to preserve the tenancy while ensuring that the Landlord receives payment of the outstanding arrears.
It is ordered that:
1. The Tenant shall pay to the Landlord $23,515.05 for arrears of rent up to May 31, 2026, and costs.
4. If the Tenant fails to make any one of the payments in accordance with this order, the Landlord may, without notice to the Tenant, apply to the LTB within 30 days of the Tenant’s breach pursuant to section 78 of the Act for an order terminating the tenancy and evicting the Tenant.
