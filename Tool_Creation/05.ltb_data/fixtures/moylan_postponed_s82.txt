[Landlord 1] and [Landlord 2] (the 'Landlords') applied for an order to terminate the tenancy and evict [Tenant 1] (the 'Tenant') because the Tenant did not pay the rent that the Tenant owes.
This application was heard by videoconference on June 24, 2026 at 3:30 p.m. The Landlord [Landlord 1], the Landlord’s representative Gail Kukor Lang, licensed paralegal and the Tenant [Tenant 1] attended the hearing.
Determinations:
1. The Landlord served the Tenant with a valid Notice to End Tenancy Early for Non-payment of Rent (N4 Notice). The Tenant did not void the notice by paying the amount of rent arrears owing by the termination date in the N4 Notice or before the date the application was filed.
3. The lawful rent is $2,030.00. It is due on the 1st day of each month.
5. The Tenant has not made any payments since the application was filed.
6. The rent arrears owing to June 30, 2026 are $6,090.00.
Section 82
10. The Tenant sought to raise several issues concerning the condition of the rental unit; however, I did not permit these issues to proceed under section 82 of the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (the 'Act') because the Tenant had not complied with the statutory disclosure requirements.
Relief from Eviction – Landlord Responsibilities
13. Section 83(3)(a) of the Act states that where a Landlord is in serious breach of their responsibilities of the Act, the Board must mandatorily refuse eviction.
16. The Tenant failed to lead any documentary or independent evidence to corroborate these allegations, such as photographs, technician reports, inspection reports, utility records or communications with the Landlord.
Relief from Eviction – Other Circumstances
20. The Tenant further testified she and her family are living in another unit temporarily; however, that finding a new permanent unit that is both affordable and suitable for her family, including the Tenant’s 4 school aged children, in the community would be challenging given the current rental market conditions. The Tenant requested a payment plan of $200.00 per month until the arrears are paid in full.
22. After considering all the circumstances, I find that it would not be unfair to postpone the eviction until August 31, 2026. I am not persuaded the Tenant would be able to commit to a payment plan, as in this case the Tenant led no documentary evidence to support her testimony regarding either her self employment income or her expenses.
It is ordered that:
1. The tenancy between the Landlord and the Tenant is terminated unless the Tenant voids this order.
